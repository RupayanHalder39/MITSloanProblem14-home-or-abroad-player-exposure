# Public release audit

Record of what went into this repository, what was deliberately left out, and
what still blocks publication.

Repository: `MITSloanProblem14-home-or-abroad-player-exposure`
Intended remote: `https://github.com/RupayanHalder39/MITSloanProblem14-home-or-abroad-player-exposure.git`
Research workspace: read-only, unmodified by this release.

## 1. Separation from the other research problem

The research workspace contains two problems.

| Workspace directory | Identity | Action |
|---|---|---|
| `problem_1` | The other research problem | **Not published.** Not referenced in any narrative, table or figure. |
| `problem_2` | MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference, Soccer track, **Problem 14** | Published |

| File | Reason for exclusion |
|---|---|
| `scripts/models/build_modeling_tables.py` | Builds the modelling table for **both** problems. Publishing it would entangle Problem 1 with Problem 14. |
| All `problem_1`-specific modules | Belong to the other problem. |

### One deliberate shared-input reference

`scripts/models/problem2_expanded_panel.py` reads
`dataset/processed/problem_1/national_team_season.csv` for the Elo series. That
is a **shared input**, not the other problem's analysis output: it is the
eloratings series normalised into the project's shape. The Stage-B code needs it
and the reference is documented in [`data/README.md`](data/README.md) rather
than hidden.

The private row-level table `dataset/processed/problem_1/M-C1_model_table.csv`
is **not** published, and no result in this repository derives from it.

**Git state.** Git was not installed on this machine and was installed via
`winget` (`Git.Git` 2.55.0.windows.3). A repository is initialised on `main` in
the release directory with all 44 files staged: no data, no archive, no
environment, no secret. Nothing is committed and nothing has been pushed. A
`.gitattributes` pins LF line endings so the verbatim code copy stays
byte-faithful regardless of any contributor's `core.autocrlf` setting. The
validator re-checks the tracked-file scope.

**Code integrity.** All 20 published source files are SHA-256 identical to the
research workspace, so the copy is verbatim. All 6 published result tables are
SHA-256 identical to their authoritative originals. Both published figures and
both supplied assets are SHA-256 identical to their sources. The package is 46
files and about 715 KB, and every published Python file compiles.

**Validator is tamper-tested.** A battery of 30 deliberate mutations was applied
in a throwaway copy, and all 30 were caught. They cover:

- either asset substituted or deleted (digest and dimension mismatch);
- an unapproved image added to `assets/`;
- a published headline number nudged by 0.7 MAE;

- the collaboration statement reworded;

- required CFF fields blanked, including an empty ORCID;
- five stray files added to the tree: a scratch note, a notebook, a row-level

Four controls that must *not* trip the checks - the unchanged package and three
pieces of legitimate wording such as "see the Author section" - stayed clean. The

## 6. Scientific claim audit

Every headline claim was re-derived from the frozen tables. All hold.

| Claim | Verified |
|---|---|
| No development model beats current Elo in Stage A from t+2 to t+5 | Yes, M1–M5 versus M0 at t+2…t+5 |
| At t+1, only the domestic opportunity index edges Elo, by a negligible margin | Yes, M4 = 42.309 versus M0 = 42.502, a margin of 0.193 MAE, reversed at every later horizon |
| Abroad-only beats domestic-only at every Stage A horizon | Yes, 47.28/54.36/65.50/75.20/80.05 versus 50.38/67.98/82.36/93.50/111.15 |
| Removing abroad worsens MAE at t+3, t+4, t+5 | Yes, +5.20, +3.28, +18.92 |
| Removing domestic improves MAE at 4 of 5 horizons, t+4 excepted | Yes, −8.14, −8.01, −1.63, +3.82, −19.41 |
| Elo + abroad improves on Elo at all five Stage B horizons | Yes, −0.128 to −0.700 and −0.197 to −0.896 |
| Abroad alone does not beat Elo | Yes, E1 is worse at t+1 to t+4 |
| Leave-one-country-out holds at t+3 | Yes, 62.70 versus 63.35, pool of 1,044 |
| Between-country is the larger component at every horizon | Yes |
| Within-country helps at all horizons except t+2, and strengthens with horizon | Yes |
| U23 and all-age profiles are near-identical | Yes, within 0.30 MAE at every horizon |
| Group B domestic is missing, never zero | Yes, 1,146 rows, zero coded zeros |
| Stage A / Stage B sample counts | Yes, 70 rows / 1,216 rows |
| Elo-baseline evaluation rows | Yes, 65/60/55/50/45 and 1,132/1,047/960/872/786 |

**Claims that were narrowed during the audit, and why.**

- *"Within-country signal helps only at t+3 to t+5."* **Rejected.** It helps at
  t+1, is slightly harmful at t+2, and strengthens with the horizon. The
  accurate statement is: smaller than the between-country component at every
  horizon, and more useful as the horizon lengthens.
- *"Sending players abroad raises Elo."* **Rejected.** Observational,
  compositional, one common slope. Not supported.
- *"U23 exposure is confirmed as a youth mechanism."* **Rejected.** The two
  features are collinear by construction, so near-identical results indicate
  non-identifiability, not confirmation.
- *"No development model beats Elo."* **Narrowed.** It is true from t+2 to
  t+5. At t+1 the domestic opportunity index is 0.193 MAE lower. The README and
  the methodology now state the exception, its size, and the fact that it
  reverses. The validator asserts both halves of this, so the claim cannot drift
  back into an overstatement.

## 7. Language and framing corrections applied

The reviewer raised two points, and both are reflected in `README.md` and
`docs/methodology.md`.

**Ruben comment 1 — motivation.** The opening no longer assumes that sending
players abroad is a good or bad idea, or that the analysis speaks to that
question. It states the question as a forecasting question and marks the
predictive-versus-causal boundary in the header, the first section, and the
limitations.

**Ruben comment 2 — Elo.** Elo is described as a **lagged match-result
outcome rather than ground truth for national-team quality**, at the top of the
README, in `docs/methodology.md` §1, and in the limitations. The superseded
motivation footnotes present in the pre-review DOCX are not carried forward.

The validator asserts the presence of these statements, and fails if a
forbidden framing — a policy recommendation, a "complete quality index" claim,
or an assertion that Elo is ground truth — reappears.

### 2. Authorship — **CONFIRMED**

**AUTHORSHIP: CONFIRMED — Rupayan Halder.**

The researcher confirmed in writing that the complete author list for this
research release is **Rupayan Halder**, and that no other person may be added as
an author. `CITATION.cff` names him alone.

**How this was previously recorded, and why it changed.** This blocker was
originally raised because the conference submission is blind-reviewed and carries
no author line, and the research workspace records no separate Problem-14 author
list. Under those conditions the repository declined to assert a complete author
list and carried the caveat in-band instead, in `CITATION.cff`, the README, and
`Handoff.md`. That earlier state was accurate at the time and is deliberately
left standing above as the historical record. It is superseded by the explicit
researcher confirmation recorded here and in those three files.

The confirmation also **ruled out** the following as sources of authorship:
reviewer comments, project files, acknowledgements, related projects, previous
papers, the SoccerSolver collaboration, and Ruben's comments on the abstract.
Reviewing and commenting on the abstract does **not** establish authorship.

SoccerSolver is a **research collaborator and acknowledgement**, and is **not**
an author. It is not listed as an author, funder, sponsor or owner anywhere, and
none of those is claimed: no evidence establishes any of those relationships. It
contributes no data to any published result. The collaboration is recorded
verbatim in the README and in `NOTICE.md`:

> This research was developed in collaboration with SoccerSolver. SoccerSolver
> currently works with more than 10 football clubs.

**Resolved.** No further action required.
